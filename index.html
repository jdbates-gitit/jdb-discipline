Tao Te Ching · Legge translation
Chapter 65

The ancients who showed their skill in practising the Tao did so, not to enlighten the people, but rather to make them simple and ignorant. The difficulty in governing the people arises from their having much knowledge. He who (tries to) govern a state by his wisdom is a scourge to it; while he who does not (try to) do so is a blessing. He who knows these two things finds in them also his model and rule. Ability to know this model and rule constitutes what we call the mysterious excellence (of a governor). Deep and far-reaching is such mysterious excellence, showing indeed its possessor as opposite to others, but leading them to a great conformity to him.

What it's pointing at

The Tao Te Ching here warns that governing—or living—through accumulated knowledge and clever schemes creates complexity and resistance. Simplicity and trust, by contrast, create natural order. The paradox is that a leader who doesn't force solutions through intellect becomes a model others naturally follow, because they sense something whole and uncontaminated in that person. This 'mysterious excellence' isn't cunning; it's the absence of interference.

Read against today

We live in an age of information overload and competing expert certainties, where every problem is met with more data, more analysis, more argument. Yet the noise has only deepened our divisions. We've come to believe that if we simply knew more, understood better, persuaded more skillfully, we could fix what's broken. But the chapter suggests the opposite: our very hunger to solve everything through intellect may be the wound itself. What if the world is crying not for more answers, but for people who have stopped grasping—people simple and grounded enough to let others be? That simplicity has become radical.

To carry today

Today, notice where you're trying to convince, control, or solve through force of knowledge. Can you set that down, even for an hour? Notice instead what happens when you simply show up without agenda—clear, unhurried, present. This is your 'mysterious excellence': not what you know, but what you've stopped needing to prove.
