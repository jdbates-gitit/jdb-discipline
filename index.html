Tao Te Ching · Legge translation
Chapter 10

When the intelligent and animal souls are held together in one embrace, they can be kept from separating. When one gives undivided attention to the (vital) breath, and brings it to the utmost degree of pliancy, he can become as a (tender) babe. When he has cleansed away the most mysterious sights (of his imagination), he can become without a flaw. In loving the people and ruling the state, cannot he proceed without any (purpose of) action? In the opening and shutting of his gates of heaven, cannot he do so as a female bird? While his intelligence reaches in every direction, cannot he (appear to) be without knowledge? (The Tao) produces (all things) and nourishes them; it produces them and does not claim them as its own; it does all, and yet does not boast of it; it presides over all, and yet does not control them. This is what is called 'The mysterious Quality' (of the Tao).

What it's pointing at

This chapter teaches that wholeness comes from integration—holding opposing forces together without forcing them. True strength is not the aggressive control we admire, but the yielding receptivity of an infant or nursing mother: alert yet undefended, effective yet unconcerned with credit. The paradox is that when you stop grasping for outcomes and release your need to be seen as the doer, you become capable of moving through the world with both clarity and grace.

Read against today

We live in an age of relentless assertion—every faction convinced of its rightness, every person compelled to broadcast their stance and claim their victory. We are exhausted by control: trying to control outcomes, narratives, others' thoughts, our own futures. This chapter whispers that all that grasping is the opposite of power. The world's noise reveals how starved we are for the quality it describes: action without the ego-stamp, care without possession, presence without performance. In a time of fracture, the chapter points to a simple undoing: stop claiming, stop boasting, stop needing to prove you know. Paradoxically, that is when real influence begins.

To carry today

Today, notice one moment where you can act without needing the outcome to prove something about you—a small kindness, a word offered, a task done for its own sake. Let that moment be enough, without requiring credit or certainty. In that single breath of non-attachment, you'll touch what the Tao is pointing to.
